# Contest exchange requirements

Research checkpoint: **18 October 2026**. This is a representative survey of organizer rules, not an exhaustive contest catalog or an accepted Clacks schema. Its purpose is to test whether additional structured values have established uses before choosing a reference representation in [issue #1 brainstorming](../docs/native-model.md).

Dedicated contest-exchange recording remains **post-minimum viable product (MVP)** under D129; operators may use the general comment in MVP. Research here does not expand that scope. Radio contacts are called QSOs below.

## What actual exchanges require

An exchange is the information each station sends during the contact. A contest association identifies the activity; it does not, by itself, preserve the exchange. The examples below separate already-modeled contact facts from additional event-specific information. “Sent” is our transmission; “received” is what the other station transmitted and we copied. `DX` ordinarily denotes distant/foreign stations; in these exchanges it is also a literal organizer-defined location marker, whose exact coverage depends on the rules.

| Contest or family | Required exchange and additional information | Important distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CQ World Wide, phone and Morse code (CW) | Signal report and CQ zone. No additional nonstandard value beyond the established contact fields. | Country multipliers are scoring results; a separate country value is not another exchanged element. [Official rules, III–IV](https://www.cqww.com/rules/). |
| CQ World Wide Prefix (WPX), phone/CW | Signal report plus a progressive **serial number**. | Serial is per contact, not a permanent station identifier. Some multi-transmitter categories use independent sequences per band. The rules start the sequence at `001`. [Official rules, IV](https://cqwpx.com/rules/). |
| American Radio Relay League (ARRL) November Sweepstakes | Serial, **precedence** (station/category code), station call, **check** (two digits of the operator's or station's first-license year), and ARRL/Radio Amateurs of Canada section. | Several separate values beyond a signal report and location. Check is constant through the event; serial changes each contact. A section is an organizational contest region, not simply a state/county synonym. [Official rules, special rules 4](https://contests.arrl.org/ContestRules/SS-Rules.pdf). |
| ARRL International DX, phone/CW | United States/Canada stations send report and state/province; other stations send report and **power**, as a number or abbreviation. | Sent and received exchange schemas can differ within the same QSO. Received power is not our transmit-power field. `KW` is a permitted representation in the organizer's example. [Official rules, exchange and special rules 4](https://contests.arrl.org/ContestRules/DX-Rules.pdf). |
| ARRL Field Day, phone/CW/digital | **Operating class**, such as `3A`, and section; foreign stations send class and `DX`. | The class combines transmitter count with an operating-class letter. It is an exchanged category, unlike many submission-only category fields. The example's additional special stations do not count toward the transmitted `3A` value. [2026 rules, 5](https://www.arrl.org/field-day-rules/). |
| All Asian DX, phone/CW | Report plus **operator age**, or `01` when the operator chooses not to disclose age. Multioperator stations use average age at the start, or `01`. | This value is an event exchange, not necessarily a person's literal age. The privacy sentinel must remain distinguishable from unknown/missing. [Japan Amateur Radio League 2026 rules, 5](https://www.jarl.org/English/4_Library/A-4-3_Contests/aadx_eng.html). |
| North American Sprint, CW/radioteletype (RTTY) | Both calls, serial, operator name, and location. | Serial is additional; name and location already exist in the native model. Exchange ordering also participates in the sprint's frequency-change operating procedure; it is not reference identity. [2026 rules, 7](https://www.ncjweb.com/Sprint-Rules.pdf). |
| Straight Key Century Club (SKCC) Weekend Sprintathon | Report, location, operator name, and **membership information**, or `none` for nonmembers. | Membership is a club reference, not a QSO serial. A nonmember marker has meaning. Award-status bonuses are separate from the minimum exchange. [Organizer rules, Exchange and Scoring](https://www.skccgroup.com/operating_activities/weekend_sprintathon/). |
| North American QRP CW Club (NAQCC) sprint | Members send report, location, and **membership number**; nonmembers send report, location, and **transmitter power**, such as `5W`. QRP denotes low-power operation. | An exchange slot can select between two different meanings. It cannot safely be interpreted as an integer without knowing membership/variant. [Organizer general sprint rules](https://naqcc.info/sprint_rules.html). |
| California QSO Party | Serial plus county for California stations; serial plus state, province/territory, or `DX` for others. Signal report is not required by this exchange rule. | Serial is additional; geographic vocabulary is event-specific. County-line stations can send multiple counties in one exchange. Leading zeros in serials are explicitly unnecessary. [2026 rules, Exchange and County-Line Station](https://www.cqp.org/Rules.html). |
| Radio Society of Great Britain (RSGB) very-high/ultra-high/super-high-frequency contests | General exchange: both calls, report, serial, and locator; individual contests may override/add requirements. | Serial normally restarts per band and activity period, with an explicit overlapping-contest exception. Some machine-generated-mode events instead require only report and four-character locator, without serial. [2026 general rules, 4](https://www.rsgbcc.org/vhf/rules/26rules/General.shtml), [2026 special rules](https://www.rsgbcc.org/cgi-bin/special.pl?rule=AFS+Super+League+2024-25&year=2026). |
| World Wide Digi, FT4/FT8 digital modes | Four-character grid square. | No new event-specific exchange value is needed merely because this is a contest. Scoring uses derived grid fields and distance; those are not extra exchanged values. [Official rules, III–IV](https://ww-digi.com/rules/). |
| RSGB Islands on the Air (IOTA) Contest | World stations send report and serial; island stations add an **IOTA reference**. | This is a useful boundary example: serial is per-contact exchange data; the island reference identifies a location-bearing program group. Mixed-mode entries use one serial sequence, not separate CW/phone sequences. [2026 rules, 5](https://www.rsgbcc.org/hf/rules/2026/riota.shtml). |

The ARRL Rookie Roundup also exchanges first name and a two-digit first-license-year check. Its landing page contains current dates alongside older eligibility examples; use it as evidence of the published exchange, not a clean versioned source for every year's eligibility. [Organizer page, rule 4](https://www.arrl.org/rookie-roundup/).

## Structural consequences supported by these examples

**Several independent named values have real uses.** A Sweepstakes exchange can contain `serial=123`, `precedence=A`, `check=79`, and `section=CT`. One undifferentiated optional identifier cannot describe their roles or apply separate validation. A future structured exchange needs either such elements or an equivalent event-defined representation. A raw exchange string can preserve text but cannot provide those capabilities without subsequent parsing.

**This does not establish a need for composite reference IDs.** A Parks on the Air (POTA) park identifier, an IOTA island-group reference, a club membership number, a serial, and a precedence are not interchangeable identifiers. A future group-driven exchange definition could describe multiple values, whether nested in an association or represented elsewhere. This survey does not force a particular Rust type layout, database separation, or a general field-schema system into MVP. It does show why “every event association will always have at most one scalar value” would be an unjustified permanent restriction if that association is also intended to hold future exchanges.

**Sent and received must remain distinguishable.** They can use different schemas (ARRL DX), different values, and different levels of completeness. An exchanged name may reuse the native recorded-operator concept; a directory-provided name is not evidence of what was exchanged. Likewise, a calculated location or known station power is not automatically evidence of the exchange copied over the air. This is a modeling inference from the organizer requirements, consistent with Clacks's existing source separation.

**Some defaults are event-wide; others vary per contact.** Checks, membership numbers and operating class are often reusable event defaults; serials are contact-specific and may use multiple sequences. Rovers or county-line operation can change or multiply geographic values. Future defaults can reduce entry effort, but changing a current default must not rewrite past exchanges. A serial's sequencing scope needs event rules; it is not automatically an account-wide contact count.

**Numeric meaning and text representation need separate consideration.** `001` can represent serial one; California explicitly permits sending `1`. A two-digit check such as `01` is not automatically the full year 2001, and All Asian's `01` has another meaning entirely. Membership identifiers and abbreviations should not be blindly parsed as numbers. Future structured recording should define which formatting is significant, which rendering is organizer-defined, and whether preserving copied text is required; this research does not settle those choices.

**One physical QSO can have event-specific interpretations.** RSGB's overlapping-contest serial exception establishes that concurrent events are an actual operating case. It does not prove that two serials must always be sent or that one exchange must be duplicated. Future support needs to associate exchange interpretation with its event rather than assume one universal exchange schema per QSO.

## Interchange and submission are separate concerns

Amateur Data Interchange Format (ADIF) 3.1.8 supplies `CONTEST_ID`, numeric `STX`/`SRX` serial fields, and `STX_STRING`/`SRX_STRING` for transmitted/received contest information. These demonstrate existing interchange roles; they do not require the native model to copy that layout or guarantee lossless representation of multiple simultaneous contest associations. [Official ADIF field definitions](https://www.adif.org/318/ADIF_318.htm#QSO_Field_STX).

The World Wide Radio Operators Foundation's Cabrillo specification distinguishes the contest identifier and submission headers (categories, operators, club, score and off-times) from per-QSO data. A submission category is therefore not automatically an exchanged value, although specific events such as Sweepstakes and Field Day explicitly exchange a category-derived code. Claimed scores and multipliers are calculations/submission claims, not extra received exchange facts. [Official Cabrillo version 3 header specification](https://wwrof.org/cabrillo/cabrillo-v3-header/).

## Remaining limits and recommendation

The evidence supports a **future event-defined, multi-element exchange capability**, including sent/received roles, conditional alternatives, significant markers and serial-sequence policies. Keep that capability conceptually distinct from reference identity; the eventual implementation may still compose them through the same data-driven group machinery. This is a research recommendation, not an accepted requirement or schema.

For the current decision, ask whether reference associations are meant to contain future exchange data or merely identify participation and reference values. Do not choose a complex schema solely because contests exchange several pieces of information; do not claim no concrete need exists either. The concrete need is established for exchanges.

This survey does not design scoring, serial allocation across simultaneously operating clients, contest editions/rules versioning, specialized traffic-report formats, or complete Cabrillo export. Those require focused future work if brought into scope. Live rule URLs may change; the cited year/version and sections identify what was examined. No licensed organizer rule text is copied wholesale here.
