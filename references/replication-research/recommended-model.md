# Recommended replication model for Clacks Log

15 October 2026. **D151 selects the vector-based direction and D154 selects timeline-level ancestry and D155 selects an acknowledged-client retention boundary; exact representation and implementation remain open.** This combines researched approaches against D136–D148. It selects no database, library, wire protocol, hash algorithm, or retention duration.

## Recommendation

Use **conservative state-based optimistic reconciliation of identity-keyed whole records, augmented by dynamic version-vector causal metadata**, with **joinable immutable service evidence**, **bidirectional anti-entropy**, and **atomic conditional acceptance**.

In ordinary terms: clients save edits locally, compare them with what they previously synchronized, and ask the user about competing changes. Version vectors help distinguish older shared copies from subsequent changes. Service evidence records what was sent, received, and processed. Synchronization exchanges missing state in either direction; the server accepts a proposed change only while the state it was compared against still matches.

- **State-based optimistic reconciliation** compares saved values after allowing disconnected edits. “Optimistic” means edits can proceed before shared acceptance; “conservative” means uncertain or competing changes are preserved for resolution rather than silently choosing a winner.
- **Identity-keyed whole records** means each contact or profile has a stable identifier, and the complete record is the unit used to decide whether both sides changed. A field-level resolution interface is still used when needed.
- **Dynamic version vectors** are actor-to-counter maps that can gain entries as writers are introduced. Comparing the counters indicates whether one copy covers another's updates or whether they contain different updates, provided the metadata remains valid.
- **Immutable evidence and joins** means retaining facts that are not edited in place and combining them without duplicating the same fact. For example, receiving the same recorded upload outcome twice does not create two outcomes.
- **Bidirectional anti-entropy** means checking copies for missing state and repairing either side. It is the catch-up mechanism, including when a restored server is behind a client.
- **Atomic conditional acceptance** means checking the compared shared state and saving the update as one indivisible operation. An intervening edit causes rejection and another client comparison.

The approaches fill separate roles. Causal metadata establishes what can be established about synchronized versions; it does not pick business winners. Client value comparison decides whether resolution is needed. Evidence joins recover facts; they do not replay their effects. Transport discovers and transfers missing state; it does not interpret conflicts. Conditional acceptance closes the race between comparison and publication.

## Mutable records and reconciliation

Model account-owned radio contacts (QSOs), tag definitions, profiles and equipment as identity-keyed record values, with deletion a record state. The QSO includes its complete tag set; logbooks are views selected by membership, not owners of contact copies. Tag/fact and different-tag changes participate in ordinary whole-record reconciliation (D243). Keep durable local working values and originals separately from synchronized accepted values. Apply the agreed whole-record rules: equal results or only one changed side need no resolution; differing changes on both sides need client-side field choices, including changes to disjoint fields. Unresolved business alternatives stay on the originating client. Intermediate local edits can coalesce.

[Conservative common-state reconciliation](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~bcpierce/papers/unisonspec.pdf) supplies the semantic basis. Tag deletion applies explicit preserve/delete-all/delete-unique QSO effects (D244). A pending QSO referencing a deleted tag remains local until explicit removal/replacement of that membership (D250); this does not recreate the tag or mark an otherwise preserved QSO deleted. Acceptance must validate relevant references as well as compared record state. These are record/reference invariants, not a filesystem representation or account-wide commit.

Common values alone are insufficient for rollback: if original and local are N, but a restored server contains predecessor P, ordinary value comparison can misinterpret P as a new one-sided edit. Causal knowledge must distinguish a surviving successor from an older copy when possible.

## Causal metadata: dynamic version vectors

**Application recommendation:** use dynamic version vectors as the baseline causal representation. Twenty simultaneously active actors would be exceptionally high. Over an account lifetime, 20 actors is reasonable, 100 or more would be extremely rare, and the expected total is almost certainly below 1,000. These are workload estimates, not architecture caps. Metadata costs must account for represented lifetime identities, including identity/incarnation churn, rather than just active clients. Dotted representations or shared knowledge plus exceptions can refine the representation if their particular event/context or storage benefits are needed; neither is automatically required merely because the baseline is a version vector.

A **causal prefix** means every update from an actor through a particular counter. For example, `{A: 3, B: 2}` covers A’s first three updates and B’s first two. Comparing with `{A: 2, B: 3}` reveals that neither covers the other. Vectors summarize these prefixes rather than retaining a node per change. A modest represented population makes componentwise comparison inexpensive and avoids ordering knowledge expiring merely because old edits are no longer retained. Accurate comparison still requires intact, truthfully interpreted metadata; payload history and originals have separate retention rules. A valid vector can establish succession for an older restored copy; neither vectors nor histories are intrinsically rollback-proof, and reused writer counters must not alias distinct events.

[Dynamic creation and retirement research](https://www.csd.uwo.ca/~hlutfiyy/Perv/Papers/petersen.pdf) supplies established lifecycle mechanisms under stated cooperation and prefix assumptions. Those assumptions must be matched to Clacks. D152 makes revocation the cutoff for automatic acceptance from the actor, excluding outstanding unsynchronized work unless manually reconciled later. Clacks therefore need not wait for the lost writer to hand off every local update. An indefinitely offline, unrevoked writer remains eligible. The paper’s cooperative final-history transfer is one published retirement mechanism, not a requirement to preserve acceptance of revoked actors’ unknown work. The chosen representation must still preserve interpretable retirement knowledge and already accepted data. This is a lifecycle obligation, not evidence that vectors are a poor fit for a small account. [Dotted/context research](https://asc.di.fct.unl.pt/~nmp/pubs/dais2014.pdf) separates version identity from past knowledge but does not by itself solve retirement or destructive rollback.

### Selected timeline ancestry and retirement

D154 selects timeline/revision references with shared ancestry/coverage records at recovery boundaries. D248 selects one scope per account for QSOs with tag sets, tag definitions/lifecycle, contact-specific service evidence, download coordination/dispositions and synchronized shared configuration. Acknowledgments and D155 collection boundaries apply at account scope. D161 still replicates the entire account to every enrolled client in MVP; selective synchronization remains post-MVP. Every accepted change passes through the server; canonical accepted-state comparisons can use these positions instead of retaining a vector dimension for every historical author. See the [selected model and collection implications](../../docs/synchronization.md#selected-timeline-level-ancestry-d154).

A boundary that establishes `(T2,205)` incorporates `(T1,200)` covers old T1 positions through 200. Later positions require comparison rather than an assumed numeric continuation. Ordinary restarts do not create actors or timelines; actual restored fork origin and later incorporated coverage must be recorded truthfully. This is a sparse history of exceptional boundaries, not a per-record version graph.

D152 revocation closes further automatic admission from an actor; no final lost-device transfer or all-client acknowledgment is required. Its accepted contributions remain represented by shared records/evidence and timeline positions. Per-actor clock/progress bookkeeping becomes collectible when no independent admission, retry, or recovery obligation depends on it; deleting actor state does not delete the contacts it authored.

Unrevoked clients stay enrolled indefinitely. A bounded change journal may require fuller-state reconciliation for an old cursor; pending originals/working values remain intact. D155 retains detailed ordering evidence from the oldest authorized client’s durably acknowledged synchronization point onward and compacts older history into an equivalent shared baseline. Across timelines, use common ancestry coverage rather than numeric minima. D156 preserves pending comparison evidence locally before acknowledgment; if that save is incomplete, the old acknowledgment remains in effect. No separate pending-edit collection pin has been justified. Surviving record references remain interpretable. An offline authorized client can delay collection indefinitely; revocation removes it from that participant set. Older restored backups below the baseline use surviving evidence and explained manual recovery when ordering is unavailable. Deletion memory and service-processing evidence remain separate requirements.

D156 requires the client to atomically persist received shared state, pending comparison evidence, and its checkpoint before acknowledgment. Pending conflicts can coexist with progress on unrelated records; checkpoint advancement must preserve their evidence.

The [recovery fit analysis](recovery-fit.md) distinguishes this weaker optimistic/manual model from stronger quorum-backed atomic-register recovery. Exact detection coverage, lineage representation and compaction, submission deduplication, and metadata repair are still open; no storage quorum or framework is selected.

### What hash histories would buy

[Hash History reconciliation](https://oceanstore.cs.berkeley.edu/publications/papers/pdf/hh_icdcs03_kang.pdf) removes replica-count dependence, recognizes independently equal hash/epoch versions (including implications for descendants), and supports ancestry/delta discovery from a shared content-based history. Its graph grows with retained modifications, and pruning can lose ordering knowledge.

For Clacks, no demonstrated advantage currently outweighs that cost. The actor population is expected to be small; every account already needs enrolled-client identities; ancestry graphs and historical delta chains are not required product features. Equal current record values already bypass human resolution. Hash histories' stronger propagation of equivalence to descendants is an additional capability, but its value for this workload has not been established. Accepting uncertainty after metadata loss is a recovery allowance, not a reason to introduce avoidable metadata loss routinely.

The published graph approach remains a valid research alternative if actual workloads show substantial identity churn or useful content-equivalence convergence. It is not the preferred representation on present evidence. Neither representation alone supplies deletion retention, replay-safe service processing, or recovery of facts lost everywhere.

## Service evidence and acceptance

Represent observations, publication outcomes with actual sent values/destination, and processing decisions as immutable identity-keyed facts. A **projection** computes current status from those facts and current record values; retaining an old positive fact does not force the current status to remain positive. Duplicate-safe union is the elementary [state-based conflict-free replicated data type (CRDT) join](https://dsf.berkeley.edu/cs286/papers/crdt-tr2011.pdf). Mutable current confirmation/status is a separate projection or record, so recording historical positive evidence does not make current status permanently positive.

Persist a processing decision with its corresponding accepted mutation atomically. [Durable completion records](https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cgi-bin/papers/rifl.pdf) provide the relevant pattern. Receiving an old processed observation must not apply enrichment again or undo a later edit. Acceptance checks expected record/dependency state atomically; failure returns to client comparison. The server validates admission without deciding human conflicts or waiting for a client to resolve them.

Evidence transfer does not itself submit an upload. Retries use current accepted QSO values under existing service rules; outcomes lost everywhere remain uncertain. Restore keeps the operator-controlled all-upload pause and explicit resume.

## Dissemination

Use bidirectional state-based anti-entropy with a bounded incremental change journal (a retained sequence of changes after known positions) for ordinary catch-up and fuller-state reconciliation when that journal is unavailable. A **snapshot** is a coherent state at a known point; its **ordered change suffix** contains changes after that point. Establish a consistent snapshot and its ordered change suffix before transitioning to live streaming; overlap is idempotent (duplicate delivery has no additional effect) and acknowledgements follow durable receipt. [Watermark-window reconciliation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.12597) is a researched alternative if the eventual storage supports its ordered-log and non-stale-read assumptions.

A **Merkle index** summarizes data in a hash tree; a **set sketch** summarizes a collection so its differences may be recovered compactly. Either can later reduce difference-discovery cost. They are optional optimizations, not the record model. An old cursor expires as an optimization; client eligibility and retained local work do not.

## Tradeoffs

| Alternative | Advantage over this recommendation | Why it is not the first choice here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hash histories | Metadata independent of actor count; content-equivalence recognition and explicit ancestry | Expected actor counts are small; retaining and traversing history adds costs, while pruning can introduce avoidable resolution prompts. No compensating workload benefit has been demonstrated |
| Plain original/local/shared values | Simplest state and comparison | Cannot distinguish an older restored synchronized value from a newly authored change without additional ordering evidence |
| Automatic record CRDT merge, last-writer-wins (LWW), or a shared multi-value register | Automatic convergence; LWW has small metadata; multi-value registers preserve alternatives | Automatic disjoint-field merge or timestamp winners violate selected behavior; shared unresolved values violate their client-local location. CRDTs remain the preferred elementary model for immutable evidence |
| Operation logs, dependency scheduling, or transformation | Preserve commands/intent and can express richer cross-record semantics | More replay/context/domain-law machinery than coalesced final-record comparison requires. Retaining commands alone does not make external effects reversible or deduplicated |
| Interval-tree or bounded clocks | Established ownership-based or fixed-population compaction | Indefinite eligible writers, reauthorization, restored installations, and arbitrary client count do not automatically satisfy exclusive-ownership or fixed-population assumptions |

The central causal-metadata cost is **represented actor dimensions and their lifecycle**, including retirement and restore-safe event identity. With the stated active and lifetime population estimates, vectors remain the preferred baseline; estimate storage and comparison costs against represented lifetime identities, not only concurrent clients. Keeping retired components may be practical in some scopes, but naive duplication across every QSO can multiply that cost substantially. The workload estimate does not remove the requirement to assess established retirement/compaction mechanisms. Naive per-record vectors repeat actor metadata; factored knowledge is a researched optimization if measurements warrant it. Permanent deletion/evidence retention remains a separate storage obligation.

This combination is a source-backed application recommendation, not a claim that one paper proves the combined application. The next design work is to specify its record/history/evidence transitions and verify them against the agreed recovery and conflict cases before implementation selection.
