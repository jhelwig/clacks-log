# Replication approaches: convergence, data models, and transfer

Primary-source analysis checked 13 October 2026 against D136–D150. This compares research models and their assumptions, not products. No clock encoding, protocol, storage engine, or bespoke composition is proposed. Formal statements below summarize cited source models; Clacks applicability is inference unless identified as an accepted requirement.

## Reading guide

**Convergence** means copies eventually reach equivalent states after learning the relevant changes. A **merge/join** is the rule used to combine their information. **Idempotent** means repeating the same input has no additional effect; **commutative** means reversing input order does not change the result; **associative** means changing grouping does not change it. Set union has all three properties: duplicate observations and delivery order do not change the resulting set.

A **conflict-free replicated data type (CRDT)** defines data and update/merge rules that converge under specified delivery assumptions. It may represent several competing values; “conflict-free” does not mean its automatic policy matches the user's desired business decision. A **projection** computes a current view from retained facts—for example, current publication status from past attempts and the current contact values.

A **tombstone** remembers a deletion after its payload is gone. **Causal context** remembers which update events have been observed. **Causal stability** means no future delivered operation can be concurrent with an event. None of these follows merely from one peer acknowledging receipt. In cost notation, `O(n)` describes growth with `n`, ignoring constant factors.

## The application contract is distinct from convergence

Let O be originally observed record values, L durable local working values, and S shared accepted values, including deleted as a possible state. Together these are the original/local/shared-value comparison inputs. D137/D138 require no resolution flow for one-sided changes or equal results; if both sides changed and differ, the client resolves fields—even disjoint-field edits. An unresolved competing business record stays local. D146 conditionally accepts the selected result only if relevant shared state still matches what the client compared.

These are business admission/comparison rules. A replication join can converge **evidence** without selecting the publishable radio contact (QSO). Conversely, converging business values automatically may violate the chosen rule. “CRDT” therefore neither proves compatibility nor means an application cannot display conflicts.

Symmetric repair must restore surviving synchronized records, deletion knowledge, and service evidence into an older server, retaining pending work. Indefinite offline return and fuller-state fallback are accepted. Unknown ordering permits clearly explained conservative resolution; genuine recovery divergence with missing originals permits two-way resolution. Reauthorization preserves retained data, but D152 requires manual reconciliation of previously unsynchronized work excluded by revocation. Uploads are operator-paused during restore; no model can recover facts absent everywhere or reverse external effects.

## Convergence frameworks

**State-based CRDT / join-semilattice.** Replicas exchange represented state and merge it. For immutable evidence, that can mean unioning sets of facts. A **join-semilattice** orders states by information content and defines the least state containing both inputs. An **inflationary** update adds information in that order, even if its user-visible projection removes an item. Formally, state X lies in a join-semilattice (S, ≤, ⊔); Here S is the set of possible states, ≤ means “contains no more information than,” and ⊔ is the join operation. Local transitions are inflationary and merge is join. Associativity, commutativity, and idempotence make duplicate/reordered state delivery harmless. Eventual communication of update information yields equivalent states. Cost depends on the datatype: a grow-only set grows with retained distinct elements; some counters summarize per replica; histories/tombstones may grow with updates. The theorem does not select a fixed-size representation. [Shapiro, Preguiça, Baquero, Zawirski, _A Comprehensive Study of Convergent and Commutative Replicated Data Types_, 2011, §2](https://dsf.berkeley.edu/cs286/papers/crdt-tr2011.pdf).

**Fit (inference):** symmetric join is naturally useful when one copy retains evidence another lacks. A server star is a communication topology, not a prohibition on this algebra. However, arbitrary QSO assignment is not inflationary in the order of user-visible values; an appropriate register/evidence lattice changes the formal state. Unrestricted joining of pending competing QSO values into shared state violates their local-only requirement. Conditional admission is not supplied by semilattice convergence.

**Operation-based CRDT.** Replicas send effects such as “increment by one” instead of the whole state. Two concurrent increments can be applied in either order; delivering one twice would still be wrong unless duplication is handled. A local prepare step produces an effect message, which remote replicas apply. Concurrent effects must commute; required delivery/order guarantees depend on the datatype. Standard causal reliable dissemination delivers effects without duplicate application; idempotent effects can relax the duplicate restriction. Replication state plus middleware buffering/deduplication replaces full-state dissemination. [Almeida, _Approaches to Conflict-free Replicated Data Types_, 2024 revision/publication, operation-based sections](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.18220v2).

**Fit (inference):** submitted operations can describe coalesced record changes rather than keystrokes. But an operation log that discards acknowledged effects is not a source for recovering an older accepted server; surviving materialized state or retained effects must suffice under the source's recovery contract. Conditional acceptance and the original/local/shared-value comparison rule remain separate. Exactly-once **effect delivery** is a middleware assumption, not exactly-once external uploads.

**Delta-state CRDT.** Send a mergeable state fragment instead of the entire state. Unlike an arbitrary command, the fragment uses the state datatype’s duplicate-safe join. A delta-mutator is an update function returning a mergeable fragment d; state X becomes X ⊔ d. Delta groups join fragments. Causal interval delivery requires the receiver to subsume the sender's pre-interval state. The anti-entropy algorithm retains state/counter durably, buffers deltas/acknowledgments, and sends full state when deltas are unavailable. [Almeida, Shoker, Baquero, _Delta State Replicated Data Types_, 2018; preprint 2016, §§5–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.01529).

**Critical proof boundary:** the source assumes immediately pre-crash durable-state recovery. Its fallback handles lost volatile buffers; reused rewound counters/acknowledgments are outside that proof. Delta-buffer collection also does not authorize deleting the lattice's causal/deletion evidence. Payload savings retain the underlying datatype's semantics; deltas are not a new QSO conflict policy.

**Pure operation-based CRDT.** Send the user operation itself, with the delivery layer supplying causal information. Keep operations whose relative order still matters and discard them when the datatype’s rules prove them redundant or stable. Prepare sends the operation without consulting datatype state. Commutative operations can update compact state directly. Noncommutative semantics use a partially ordered operation log (PO-Log), tagged causal delivery, and datatype-specific obsolescence/redundancy relations. Causal-stability notifications permit further compaction. [Baquero, Almeida, Shoker, _Pure Operation-Based Replicated Data Types_, 2017 preprint, §§3–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.04469).

State/cost comprises the PO-Log plus delivery metadata until operations become redundant/stable. Causal stability means future delivered operations cannot be concurrent with a given event; it is not simply “this peer acknowledged receipt.” **Fit (inference):** an indefinitely disconnected writer can delay stability. Removing it from a delivery group requires lifecycle semantics; automatic elapsed-time retirement is not accepted here. A retained reauthorized client and restored server must remain meaningful after compaction. These papers do not supply that complete lifecycle unchanged.

## Datatypes, causal contexts, and business projection

**Grow-only evidence and two-phase sets.** A grow-only set (G-set) only adds elements. A two-phase set keeps added and removed identities separately and never allows a removed identity to return. G-set state E is a set; insert and merge use union. Storage grows with the number of retained distinct facts, with no deletion. Two-phase set state (A,R) joins each component by union; query is A minus R and a removed identity cannot be re-added. [2011 CRDT study, set catalog](https://dsf.berkeley.edu/cs286/papers/crdt-tr2011.pdf). These can preserve immutable observations/decisions or permanent deletion identities. They do not resolve competing mutable records. Collection of facts needs a separate proof that lost evidence is no longer required; two-phase deletion cannot by itself represent the required delete-versus-changed-record resolution.

**Observed-remove sets and causal dot stores.** A **dot** names an individual update. An insertion receives a distinct dot, conventionally (replica,counter). State records live dots plus observed causal context. Removal suppresses observed insertions; a concurrent unseen insertion survives add-wins merge. A dot absent from the store but present in the context carries removal knowledge. The optimized observed-remove set (OR-set) replaces per-removal tombstones with summarized causal knowledge, not with no deletion evidence. Metadata includes active insertion tags and replica summaries rather than an unbounded tombstone per removed element. [Bieniusa et al., _An Optimized Conflict-Free Replicated Set_, 2012, §§4–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.3368).

**Fit (inference):** useful demonstration that “retain deletion knowledge” need not mean retaining all original payloads or tombstones forever. It still depends on unique event identities and valid causal summaries. Concurrent add-wins/remove-wins semantics are explicit policy choices, neither identical to Clacks's deletion-versus-edit resolution. Removing actor summaries or reusing counters is not justified by having optimized tombstones.

**Registers and maps.** A **register** is a replicated value slot; a **map** associates keys with values. A **last-writer-wins (LWW) register** selects the assignment with the highest ordering tag, such as a timestamp plus a tie-breaker. A **multi-value (MV) register** retains causally maximal assignments: an assignment supersedes the predecessors it observed, while concurrent assignments remain. Its size depends on concurrent live assignments plus causal metadata; equality of values and equality of assignment events differ. Maps compose nested datatypes; causal maps also track removal contexts. Using independent field registers would merge disjoint field edits automatically. [Almeida 2024, register/data-model discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.18220v2); [Almeida–Shoker–Baquero 2018, causal datatype/map catalog](https://webarchive.di.uminho.pt/haslab.uminho.pt/ashoker/files/deltacrdt_0.pdf).

**Fit (inference):** whole-record MV registers can preserve distinct concurrent record values, rather than automatically merge fields. But putting unresolved values in a shared MV register breaches their required client-local location; an application conflict interface alone does not cure that. LWW silently selects a winner and mismatches the rule. A whole-record equality check can suppress a flow even if multiple equivalent assignments remain as evidence. Nested map deletion/update semantics must be assessed explicitly: Clacks contacts are account-owned records with tag sets, not children of tag records. D244 selects whether tag deletion preserves contacts or marks specified contacts deleted. D250 keeps a pending QSO with a deleted-tag reference local until explicit removal/replacement, while ordinary QSO deletion/edit conflicts remain distinct.

The collection of causal datatypes pairs a dot store with context: a dot set, dot-to-value function, or key-to-dot-store map. Its joins encode causal removals: omission of a previously observed event means removal, whereas an event never observed may still be missing from transfer. These are existing generic models, not a proposed sparse clock/lifecycle. Event summaries cannot recreate missing record bodies or comparison originals.

## Admission and semantic alternatives

**Optimistic state transfer / reconciliation.** Formal object state includes values and conflict-detection preconditions/history summaries; disconnected edits alter local state and reconciliation detects/settles divergence. Whole-object state transfer need only carry final values, while operation transfer carries prescriptions. Ordering, propagation, conflict detection, and commitment are separate design dimensions. [Saito and Shapiro, _Optimistic Replication_, 2005, §§1–6](https://dsf.berkeley.edu/cs286/papers/optimistic-csur2005.pdf). Metadata cost varies with masters/history; fixed-age tombstone expiry assumes bounds on stale return.

**Fit (inference):** this family is semantically closer to durable coalesced working records and client resolution than automatic field merge. The taxonomy is not a complete proved protocol for Clacks. Original values, safe conditional admission, bidirectional rollback repair, and retention must be supplied by an actual candidate model's documented contract, not inferred from the label.

**Semantic optimistic operation replication.** Bayou state combines an ordered write log, materialized tuples, and rollback support; writes carry dependency checks and merge procedures, are tentative until committed, and may be undone/replayed. [Terry et al., _Managing Update Conflicts in Bayou_, 1995, §§3–6](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs6464/2009sp/papers/bayou.pdf). Log retention/stability bounds storage. Application-aware checks are reusable research, but server-visible tentative competing records and automatic merge/replay mismatch this contract. Database replay must not replay publications.

**Coordination/admission reasoning.** Invariant confluence asks whether independently reachable valid states remain valid under merge; when not, coordination or changed semantics is needed under its model. [Bailis et al., _Coordination Avoidance in Database Systems_, 2014/2015, §4](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol8/p185-bailis.pdf). This distinguishes storage convergence from application correctness. D146's online conditional acceptance is compatible with available offline drafts: recording a draft is not claiming a globally accepted write. No theorem here proves D137 is an invariant-confluent merge policy or selects an admission implementation.

## Anti-entropy and transfer are orthogonal

**Anti-entropy** checks for missing state and repairs it; live notifications alone cannot repair every missed update. A **Merkle tree** summarizes data in a hierarchy of hashes so peers can locate differing ranges without transferring everything. A **set sketch** is a compact summary that can sometimes recover differences directly. An **ordered change suffix** is the sequence of changes after a snapshot’s recorded position; a **watermark** marks such a position or boundary. These mechanisms discover or transport differences; they do not choose business winners.

| Approach and formal transferred state | Transitions / assumptions / cost | Meaning for Clacks (inference) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Periodic pairwise state anti-entropy | Compare states and transfer differences; eventual communication is required. Full comparisons cost O(total compared state). | Repair can run in both star directions; streaming alone cannot repair missed state. Resolution is separate. |
| Digest/Merkle tree | Hash canonical objects/ranges; compare roots, descend unequal subtrees, fetch differing payloads. Cost depends on differences and tree structure; retain/update hashes. | Equality discovery, not ancestry, acceptance, recoverable bytes, or a conflict policy. Include deletion/service evidence in whatever is being compared. |
| invertible Bloom lookup table (IBLT)/set reconciliation | Subtract sketches of descriptor sets and decode their difference probabilistically below capacity; retain/sketch entries proportional to capacity. Oversized differences require larger sketches/exact fallback. | Include value/state descriptors, not only entity IDs. Decode failure is not proof of synchronization; found evidence still needs interpretation. |
| State versus log transfer | Send current materialized state or retained transitions after a position. Logs need deduplication, ordering, and replay retention; snapshots can replace replay suffixes under specified contracts. | Coalescing working edits is possible with state transfer. A log cursor is not stable business identity after rollback; full state is not permission to overwrite surviving newer evidence. |
| Snapshot plus live changes | Coordinate a snapshot/chunk boundary with changes, using consistent positions or watermark windows and overlap handling. | Prevents catch-up/stream gaps; direction and acceptance policy remain separate. |

Primary evidence: [Demers et al., _Epidemic Algorithms for Replicated Database Maintenance_, 1987](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~bcpierce/courses/dd/papers/demers-epidemic.pdf); [DeCandia et al., _Dynamo_, 2007, §4.7](https://people.csail.mit.edu/alinush/6.824-spring-2015/papers/dynamo.pdf); [Goodrich–Mitzenmacher, _Invertible Bloom Lookup Tables_, 2011](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1101.2245); Saito–Shapiro 2005 above; [Andreakis–Papapanagiotou, _DBLog_, 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.12597). These are transport/discovery precedents, not product recommendations.

## Stability, retention, and replay-safe effects

Causal stability, dissemination acknowledgment, durable acceptance, and external success are different facts. Fixed-time removal of deletion evidence can resurrect stale copies (Demers 1987). Waiting for all possible writers can block garbage collection (GC) indefinitely. Full-state fallback can allow transfer-log collection while **not** allowing arbitrary deletion of causal/deletion/service evidence. Conservative resolution for unknown order relaxes how much ordering must remain; it does not make absence distinguishable from deletion. Ordinary pending originals remain required even though recovery originals may be absent.

A restored replica can violate the assumed monotone durable-state history of a published model. A reauthorized retained writer similarly cannot be treated as a fresh unrelated dataset merely to satisfy a model's membership assumption. These are explicit fit tests, not new lifecycle proposals.

Joining immutable sent-payload/outcome/observation/processing evidence can be idempotent while the derived business action is not. A repeated observation must not reapply enrichment to a later corrected QSO. Durable processed-message/completion evidence is substantive state: [Helland, _Life Beyond Distributed Transactions_, 2007](https://www.cidrdb.org/cidr2007/papers/cidr07p15.pdf); [Lee et al., _Implementing Linearizability at Large Scale and Low Latency_, 2015](https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cgi-bin/papers/rifl.pdf). The latter's completion records/leases establish a particular remote procedure call (RPC) contract, not indefinite offline enrollment or exactly-once Logbook of The World (LoTW)/QRZ effects. Lost completion evidence cannot be fabricated. A new retry uses current accepted values; a recovered success covers the values actually sent.
